main_message_paragraphs:
  - "The parable of the ten bridesmaids teaches that readiness for Jesus&rsquo;s return is not something we can improvise at the last moment, but something formed over time through a genuine, persevering relationship with Him. We are wise to live with consistent, sustained spiritual readiness &ndash; living faithfully, staying awake to our calling, and cultivating intimacy with Jesus &ndash; in anticipation of His return. Though the bridegroom may delay longer than expected, this parable issues a merciful warning to never settle for just outward religious activity while our hearts remain far from God, but rather to cultivate an inner life of love, trust, and obedience through dependence on the Spirit. When Jesus returns and the door finally closes, only those who have become ready will enter."

dt_intro_paragraphs:
  - "Jesus tells this parable while teaching about His return, knowing there will be a delay. Life will feel normal, and urgency will fade, causing most men to assume they have time. This is where men drift&mdash;not necessarily through outright rejection but through neglect and delay. This parable confronts the assumption that readiness can be addressed later, showing that what is built over time ultimately matters."

dt_closing_paragraphs:
  - "Jesus makes it clear that the dividing line is relationship. Through Him, men are invited into a real, ongoing relationship with God&mdash;not surface-level belief, but being known by Him. The warning is direct: there will be a moment when preparation is no longer possible. The door will close, and what has been built will be revealed. The Gospel is not just an invitation to believe&mdash;it is an invitation to walk with God over time. The question is whether a man will respond now or assume he has more time."
key_points:

  - "What sustains you in the moment is the power and presence of the Spirit and a genuine relationship with God that is cultivated and built over time; readiness cannot be rushed or borrowed."
  - "The dividing line is not appearance or proximity, but whether you have a real relationship with Jesus before the door closes."
