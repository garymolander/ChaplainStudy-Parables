main_message_paragraphs:
  - God&rsquo;s mercy and grace toward lost sinners is extravagant, undeserved,
    and even scandalous. Through the two sons, Jesus reveals that there are two
    ways to be lost and far from the Father &mdash; by running away in open sin
    like the younger son, or by staying home and trying to earn God&rsquo;s
    favor like the older son. Yet both must be rescued by the same astounding
    grace. God&rsquo;s lavish compassion welcomes both sons home through grace
    and repentant faith.

discovery_questions:

    relationship?
  - Do you tend to run from God or try to prove yourself to Him? Why?
dt_intro_paragraphs:
  - "The tension in this parable comes from who Jesus is addressing. He&rsquo;s
    speaking to religious people who believed closeness to God was earned
    through discipline, behavior, and separation from those who didn&rsquo;t
    measure up. They were frustrated that Jesus welcomed sinners because it
    challenged their system. In their minds, proximity to God was achieved.
    Jesus confronts that directly&mdash;not with an argument, but with a story
    that reveals a different reality: proximity to God is received, not earned."
cards_layout: 3-col
cards:
  - eyebrow: PARABLE ONE
    title: The Younger Son
    body: "The Younger Son represents open rebellion. He demands what he believes
      belongs to him, walks away from the father, and builds a life on his own
      terms. At first, it looks like freedom&mdash;no authority, no limits, no
      accountability&mdash;but that version of freedom doesn&rsquo;t last. It
      leads to emptiness, desperation, and collapse. This is the pattern of sin:
      it promises control but slowly strips a man of what matters. When he hits
      bottom, he decides to return, but even then, his thinking is off. He
      prepares an apology speech, assuming he has to earn his way back, seeing
      himself no longer as a son but as a worker trying to survive. Yet before
      he can even finish his apology, the father runs to him. In that culture, a
      man of status did not run&mdash;it was undignified&mdash;but the father
      disregarded that cost to his reputation willingly, restoring his son
      without condition. He places a robe on him, a ring on his hand, and
      sandals on his feet, immediately reinstating his identity as a son. This
      is not earned restoration. This is grace."
  - eyebrow: PARABLE TWO
    title: The Older Son
    body: The Older Son represents a different kind of lostness. He never leaves
      physically, and from the outside, his life looks right. He is disciplined,
      consistent, and obedient&mdash;the kind of man others respect. But when
      grace is extended to his brother, what is underneath is exposed. He is
      angry, not because something wrong happened, but because something
      unearned was given. His relationship with the father was never rooted in
      love&mdash;it was transactional. He believed his effort should produce a
      reward, and when it didn&rsquo;t give him an advantage, it revealed his
      heart. He didn&rsquo;t run from the father, but he didn&rsquo;t truly know
      or love him. His obedience was an attempt to earn righteousness.
  - eyebrow: THE FATHER
    title: The Father
    body: This parable exposes two different spiritual conditions of men. A man can
      rebel openly like the younger son or perform quietly like the older son,
      and both paths can leave him equally distant from God. One builds life on
      independence. The other builds life on effort.
dt_closing_paragraphs:
  - And yet, the father moves toward both. He runs to the younger son and goes
    out to plead with the older son. He does not wait for either of them to fix
    themselves&mdash;he initiates restoration. This is the heart of the gospel.
    God does not sit back waiting for men to clean themselves up. Through Jesus,
    He moves toward them, absorbing the cost Himself to bring them back into
    relationship&mdash;not as servants trying to prove their worth, but as sons
    who receive it.
key_points:
  - Closeness to God is not earned through rebellion or performance&mdash;it is
    received through grace.
  - You can be far from God through open sin or through outward obedience rooted
    in a transactional mindset.
  - The Father&rsquo;s response reveals His compassion, kindness, and lavish
    grace&mdash;He runs toward those who recognize their need, repent, and come
    home.
  - The Father moves toward you first, and you are not home until you stop
    proving yourself and receive what He freely gives.
discussion_questions:
  - Where are you chasing freedom outside of God right now, and what has that
    pursuit produced in your life?
  - Why do people try to earn their way back instead of receiving and accepting
    His grace?
  - Do you ever feel like God owes you something because of your effort?
  - How do you respond when others receive grace you don&rsquo;t think they
    deserve?
  - Which son do you relate to more right now&mdash;and why?
  - What is keeping you from fully coming home to God?
personal_reflection_paragraphs:
  - Most men live in one of two lanes with God&mdash;you either run or you
    perform. You chase control, success, or escape, thinking freedom is found
    outside of God. Or you stay disciplined and consistent, but begin to believe
    your effort earns you something with God. Both miss the point. You
    don&rsquo;t earn your way back to God, and you don&rsquo;t outperform your
    need for Him. Real strength is not proving yourself&mdash;it&rsquo;s
    surrendering. The moment you stop running and start receiving grace is the
    moment your life actually begins to change.

  - Where does pride or quiet judgment show up in your life like the older son?
  - In your environment (team, work, family), how can you lead with grace this
    week?
  - What is one clear step you will take this week to move toward God?
