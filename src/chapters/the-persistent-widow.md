  - "Jesus tells this parable with a clear purpose: don&rsquo;t give up. He knows there will be seasons where prayers feel unanswered, and progress feels nonexistent. In those moments, most men don&rsquo;t walk away completely&mdash;they slowly disengage. This is not about prayer technique; it is about trust and endurance. The real issue is not whether God hears, but whether a man will stay engaged with hope and perseverance when he doesn&rsquo;t see results."

    title: "The Unjust Judge"
    body: "The unjust judge represents indifference and a lack of character. He does not care about God or people, and he has no internal motivation to do what is right. The only reason he responds is that persistence becomes a burden to him. Jesus uses this contrast to make a point&mdash;if a corrupt and self-serving man will eventually respond, how much more will God respond, who is just, attentive, and good."
  - eyebrow: "PARABLE TWO"
    title: "The Persistent Widow"
    body: "The persistent widow represents dependence and endurance. She has no power, leverage, or control, but she keeps coming back. She does not try to manipulate the situation or take control&mdash;she stays in a position of need. Her persistence is rooted in trust and hope. She refuses to walk away, even when nothing changes. This is the model Jesus puts in front of men&mdash;consistent dependence, not control."
dt_closing_paragraphs:
  - "Jesus makes it clear that God is not like the unjust judge&mdash;He is righteous, present, and fully aware. Through Jesus, men are brought into a relationship with a Father who hears and responds according to His will. The cross proves God is not indifferent, and the resurrection proves He is not powerless. Delay is not absence&mdash;it is an invitation to trust. The real question is not whether God will act, but whether He will find a man who keeps coming to Him in faith&mdash;trusting His justice instead of taking matters into his own hands."
