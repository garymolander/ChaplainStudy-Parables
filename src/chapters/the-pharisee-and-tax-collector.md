dt_intro_paragraphs:
  - "Jesus tells this parable to people who believed they were right with God because of how they lived. These were disciplined, moral men who built their identity on doing the right things while quietly looking down on others. This is not a story about obvious failure&mdash;it is about hidden pride. Jesus exposes the reality that a man can look strong on the outside and still be completely disconnected from God if his confidence is in himself."

cards:

    title: "The Pharisee"
    body: "The Pharisee represents outward strength with inward pride. He is disciplined, consistent, and respected, and by every external measure, his life looks right. But when he prays, he is not seeking God&mdash;he is presenting himself. His confidence is built on performance, and he measures his worth by comparing himself to other men. That comparison fuels pride and creates distance from God. A man can build a strong life externally and still be completely disconnected if his identity is rooted in what he does."
  - eyebrow: "PARABLE TWO"
    title: "The Tax Collector"
    body: "The tax collector represents honest humility. He is not respected and carries the weight of his sin, but he does not hide it or manage it. He comes to God aware of his need, without comparison or defense. His prayer is simple: &lsquo;Be merciful to me.&rsquo; He brings nothing but his need, and Jesus makes it clear that this is the man who is made right with God is humble in repentance and faith, not performance, is what opens the door to grace."
dt_closing_paragraphs:
  - "Jesus makes it clear that the humble man is justified, not the impressive one. To be justified means to be declared right before God, not because of performance, but because of dependence. This is the heart of the gospel. Through Jesus, the way is opened for men not to earn their place, but to receive it. Pride will always block grace because it convinces a man he doesn&rsquo;t need it. But when a man comes honestly, humbly, and surrendered, grace meets him there. The question is not how strong you look, but how you come before God."
