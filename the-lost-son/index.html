The Lost Son

Study #3—The Parables of Jesus

Download Printable PDF

Scripture

Luke 15:11–31

The Lost Son

Tap to read passage →

Main Message

God’s mercy and grace toward lost sinners is extravagant, undeserved, and even scandalous. Through the two sons, Jesus reveals that there are two ways to be lost and far from the Father — by running away in open sin like the younger son, or by staying home and trying to earn God’s favor like the older son. Yet both must be rescued by the same astounding grace. God’s lavish compassion welcomes both sons home through grace and repentant faith.

“God’s lavish compassion welcomes both sons home through grace and repentant faith.”

Discovery

Discovery Questions

  1. Where are you most tempted to relate to God based on performance instead of relationship?
  2. Do you tend to run from God or try to prove yourself to Him? Why?

Discovering Truth

The tension in this parable comes from who Jesus is addressing. He’s speaking to religious men who believed closeness to God was earned through discipline, behavior, and separation from those who didn’t measure up. They were frustrated that Jesus welcomed sinners because it challenged their system. In their minds, proximity to God was achieved. Jesus confronts that directly—not with an argument, but with a story that reveals a different reality: proximity to God is received, not earned.

PARABLE ONE

The Younger Son

The Younger Son represents open rebellion. He demands what he believes belongs to him, walks away from the father, and builds a life on his own terms. At first, it looks like freedom—no authority, no limits, no accountability—but that version of freedom doesn’t last. It leads to emptiness, desperation, and collapse. This is the pattern of sin: it promises control but slowly strips a man of what matters. When he hits bottom, he decides to return, but even then, his thinking is off. He prepares an apology speech, assuming he has to earn his way back, seeing himself no longer as a son but as a worker trying to survive. Yet before he can even finish his apology, the father runs to him. In that culture, a man of status did not run—it was undignified—but the father disregarded that cost to his reputation willingly, restoring his son without condition. He places a robe on him, a ring on his hand, and sandals on his feet, immediately reinstating his identity as a son. This is not earned restoration. This is grace.

PARABLE TWO

The Older Son

The Older Son represents a different kind of lostness. He never leaves physically, and from the outside, his life looks right. He is disciplined, consistent, and obedient—the kind of man others respect. But when grace is extended to his brother, what is underneath is exposed. He is angry, not because something wrong happened, but because something unearned was given. His relationship with the father was never rooted in love—it was transactional. He believed his effort should produce a reward, and when it didn’t give him an advantage, it revealed his heart. He didn’t run from the father, but he didn’t truly know or love him. His obedience was an attempt to earn righteousness.

THE FATHER

The Father

This parable exposes two different spiritual conditions of men. A man can rebel openly like the younger son or perform quietly like the older son, and both paths can leave him equally distant from God. One builds life on independence. The other builds life on effort.

And yet, the father moves toward both. He runs to the younger son and goes out to plead with the older son. He does not wait for either of them to fix themselves—he initiates restoration. This is the heart of the gospel. God does not sit back waiting for men to clean themselves up. Through Jesus, He moves toward them, absorbing the cost Himself to bring them back into relationship—not as servants trying to prove their worth, but as sons who receive it.

Key Points

  • Closeness to God is not earned through rebellion or performance—it is received through grace.
  • You can be far from God through open sin or through outward obedience rooted in a transactional mindset.
  • The Father’s response reveals His compassion, kindness, and lavish grace—He runs toward those who recognize their need, repent, and come home.
  • The Father moves toward you first, and you are not home until you stop proving yourself and receive what He freely gives.

Discuss

Group Discussion Questions

  1. Where are you chasing freedom outside of God right now, and what has that pursuit produced in your life?
  2. Why do people try to earn their way back instead of receiving and accepting His grace?
  3. Do you ever feel like God owes you something because of your effort?
  4. How do you respond when others receive grace you don’t think they deserve?
  5. Which son do you relate to more right now—and why?
  6. What is keeping you from fully coming home to God?

Personal Reflection

Most men live in one of two lanes with God—you either run or you perform. You chase control, success, or escape, thinking freedom is found outside of God. Or you stay disciplined and consistent, but begin to believe your effort earns you something with God. Both miss the point. You don’t earn your way back to God, and you don’t outperform your need for Him. Real strength is not proving yourself—it’s surrendering. The moment you stop running and start receiving grace is the moment your life actually begins to change.

“Real strength is not proving yourself — it’s surrendering.”

Reflect

Personal Reflection Questions

  1. Where are you currently trying to prove your worth instead of receiving it from God?
  2. What is one area of your life where you’ve been running instead of returning?
  3. Where does pride or quiet judgment show up in your life like the older son?
  4. In your environment (team, work, family), how can you lead with grace this week?
  5. What is one clear step you will take this week to move toward God?

The Parable of the Prodigal Son

11And he said, “There was a man who had two sons.

12And the younger of them said to his father, ‘Father, give me the share of property that is coming to me.’ And he divided his property between them.

13Not many days later, the younger son gathered all he had and took a journey into a far country, and there he squandered his property in reckless living.

14And when he had spent everything, a severe famine arose in that country, and he began to be in need.

15So he went and hired himself out to one of the citizens of that country, who sent him into his fields to feed pigs.

16And he was longing to be fed with the pods that the pigs ate, and no one gave him anything.

17“But when he came to himself, he said, ‘How many of my father’s hired servants have more than enough bread, but I perish here with hunger!

18I will arise and go to my father, and I will say to him, “Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you.

19I am no longer worthy to be called your son. Treat me as one of your hired servants.”’

20And he arose and came to his father. But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and felt compassion, and ran and embraced him and kissed him.

21And the son said to him, ‘Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son.’

22But the father said to his servants, ‘Bring quickly the best robe, and put it on him, and put a ring on his hand, and shoes on his feet.

23And bring the fattened calf and kill it, and let us eat and celebrate.

24For this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found.’ And they began to celebrate.

25“Now his older son was in the field, and as he came and drew near to the house, he heard music and dancing.

26And he called one of the servants and asked what these things meant.

27And he said to him, ‘Your brother has come, and your father has killed the fattened calf, because he has received him back safe and sound.’

28But he was angry and refused to go in. His father came out and entreated him,

29but he answered his father, ‘Look, these many years I have served you, and I never disobeyed your command, yet you never gave me a young goat, that I might celebrate with my friends.

30But when this son of yours came, who has devoured your property with prostitutes, you killed the fattened calf for him!’

31And he said to him, ‘Son, you are always with me, and all that is mine is yours.
